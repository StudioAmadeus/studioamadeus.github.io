# The self-catches gallery

The exhibits here are my favorite part of the whole project: the times
the system caught its own authors — including me, including the AIs that
built it. A machine that only catches the market is a backtest. A machine
that catches its makers is a governance system. Every entry below is on
the internal record with dates and receipts; these are the plain-words
versions.

## The audit that debugged itself on night one

The first time we ran the self-audit — fifty-one checks, built to
fact-check the system against its own claims — it found a real data bug
that would have quietly hurt us four days later. Good start. Then it
found a second bug: an inverted comparison inside one of its own checks.
The auditor's first catch of the week was the auditor. We fixed both and
kept the habit.

## The check that corrected its own explanation

We added a governance check that makes sure every numbered decision cited
anywhere in the system actually exists in the record — no phantom
citations. It passed its first live run, then corrected its own
explanatory comment within the hour, because the comment describing the
check was itself slightly wrong. The machine proofreads the machine.

## The audit that failed the very session that widened it

During the week we expanded the machine to watch basically all of crypto,
the nightly governance scan FAILED — on the session's own paperwork. A
queue entry had wrapped across two lines and broke the format the scanner
expects. The scanner was right. We fixed the paperwork, recorded the trap,
and moved on. It has no idea how big a week it interrupted, and that's the
point: it doesn't grade effort, it grades the record.

## The gate that refused its own school

Everything public on this site passes an automated publication gate — no
keys, no configs, no positions, no advice-shaped language. First time we
pointed it at the machine's own teaching library, it refused one of the
lessons. The library the gate quotes from failed the gate. So now the
publishing side only draws from lessons that pass, and the refused one
stays internal. The rule didn't bend for the house it lives in.

## The census that lasted two hours

The day we widened the exchange watchlist, the first census looked
clean — until I actually read the receipts window the ruling required and
found SHIB excluded by a volume floor at $713k against a $1M line. The
easy fix was lowering the floor. The honest fix, which is what we ruled,
was admitting the floor itself was the wrong idea and rebuilding the
screen as exclusions-only. The census stood for two hours. The receipts
outlived it.
